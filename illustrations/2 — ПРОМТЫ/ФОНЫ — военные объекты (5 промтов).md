# ФОНЫ — ВОЕННЫЕ ОБЪЕКТЫ (5 промтов, без домов и без дорог)

**Что это.** Пять боевых полей, в которых **нет ни одного жилого или гражданского здания и ни одной дороги**. Всё построенное в кадре — низкие военные работы: земляные капониры, полузаглублённые бункеры, брустверы, бетонные площадки, обваловки, взрывозащитные стенки, надолбы. Ни одна из них не имеет этажей, окон и скатной крыши — всё низкое, читается планом и даёт короткую тень.

**Бой остался:** воронки, гарь вокруг них, обвалившиеся траншеи, разбитые бетонные плиты. Техники по-прежнему нет нигде — только один-два мелких вкопанных обломка у края кадра.

---

## 1. Заброшенная военная база

Капониры, полузаглублённые бункеры, бетонная площадка и обваловка. Ни жилых домов, ни дорог.

```
STYLE, NON-NEGOTIABLE: cel-shaded comic illustration — flat colour fills in two clean tonal steps, crisp shadow shapes, ALMOST NO VISIBLE LINEWORK — THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE IN THIS PROMPT: shapes are separated by a CHANGE OF TONE, not by drawn lines. Any line that exists at all is the same sandy grey as the surface beside it and only ONE shade darker — so faint it is barely detectable, and at a glance it is not read as a line at all. NO dark outline anywhere: no black, no charcoal, no grey contour, no bold silhouette line around any object, nothing that looks like an ink drawing over the colour. IF THE OUTLINES CAN BE CLEARLY SEEN THEY ARE ALREADY TOO DARK — when in doubt make them fainter still. It is CORRECT for this picture to look soft, pale and almost lineless: it is a background under printed game pieces, and the pieces must be the only crisp thing on the table. The only thing allowed to be clearly darker is burnt ground). A drawn illustration, NOT a photograph and NOT a 3D render: no hatching, stippling, speckle, texture, grain or gradients; sharp even focus, no blur; flat quiet colour areas, not a drawn-and-outlined look; no text or numbers; art runs off all four edges, no frame.

CAMERA — NADIR ORTHOPHOTO, ZERO TILT: the camera is exactly overhead, aimed straight down at the ground, EVERY object is seen as its ROOF PLAN or DECK PLAN and nothing else. NOT ONE VERTICAL FACE IS VISIBLE ANYWHERE — no wall, not even a thin sliver along an edge. If any wall of any building can be seen, the camera is wrong. A multi-storey building is a flat roof outline with its rooftop machinery seen from directly above, and NOTHING of its facade. HEIGHT IS TOLD ONLY BY THE CAST SHADOW beside each object, never by showing a side. ALL lines strictly parallel, no perspective, no vanishing point, no horizon, no sky, uniform scale across the entire frame.

LIGHTING: flat, even, diffuse overhead light, as under a bright overcast sky; every object casts ONE short pale shadow, all on the same side; that shadow is the only clue to height.

VALUE WINDOW, STRICT, WITH ONE EXCEPTION: apart from burnt ground, the whole image lives between light and medium tones — the darkest tone is a medium grey, no dark silhouettes or masses, no deep shadows; ground, asphalt, openings and cast shadows all sit mid-range; the faint outlines are barely darker than the fills. THE ONE EXCEPTION IS BURN: scorch scars, burn fields and the halos around the worst hits go a MUTED DEEP GREY-BROWN SOOT TONE — clearly darker than everything else in the picture, but NEVER black, never hard charcoal and never a solid dark mass; dark enough to read at a glance and no darker. Nothing else is permitted to be that dark. This is a BACKGROUND for printed game pieces: quiet in tone, no focal point — but quiet applies to TONE ONLY, and materials, edges and objects stay sharply separated and legible.

COLOUR: muted and desaturated — faded rust-brown on torn metal; olive-grey and pale slate on torn metal panels, mid-toned, never black-green; pale concrete grey and weathered bone on structures; low-key amber on markings. Low in saturation, high in value: soft, never vivid, never dark, no saturated green.

CONTENTS OF THIS FIELD, a counted list — draw these and nothing more: bare dust-grey earth over most of the frame, open and quiet; a GROUP of FIVE EARTH REVETMENTS in a loose irregular cluster at one side, each a thick horseshoe of banked spoil with a flat floor and a ramp at the open end, all facing slightly different ways; TWO low half-sunk CONCRETE BUNKERS near them, their flat roofs showing as plain thick rectangles with stepped entry trenches cutting in; ONE large CONCRETE HARDSTAND APRON of big slabs with straight joints and faded bay markings, sand drifted over one corner; ONE long low BLAST WALL running past the apron; FOUR shell craters, two of them overlapping inside the revetment cluster; churned drag scars leading between the works.

SCALE IS SET BY COMPARING OBJECTS WITH EACH OTHER: a revetment floor is wide enough to hold something the size of a bunker roof, the hardstand apron is several revetments across, and a heavy shell crater is wider than a revetment bank is thick.

DENSITY — THIS IS A BACKGROUND, NOT AN ILLUSTRATION, AND IT MUST BE SPARSE: the listed objects are ALL that appear, they are few, and they stand far apart, with AT LEAST HALF THE FRAME carrying nothing but plain quiet surface. THE OPEN GROUND IS EMPTY: no bushes or grass tufts, no loose rocks, no debris, no small marks, no speckle, no busy detail of any kind — bare sand, earth or pavement in large flat shapes only. A crowded or speckled field is a failure. Each object drawn is fully drawn with its real construction — no squiggles or filler; if it cannot be drawn clearly at this size, leave bare ground.

BATTLE READS AS BURN, NOT AS HOLES: the clearest sign that fighting happened here is SCORCHING, not the shape of the pits. Long dark scorch scars spray outward from each impact point as flat radiating shapes,; broad burn fields with ragged uneven edges blacken whole areas around the worst hits; THESE BURNT SHAPES ARE THE DARKEST THINGS IN THE PICTURE, but they are a MUTED DEEP GREY-BROWN SOOT TONE, never black and never hard charcoal — dark enough to read clearly against the pale ground and no darker, thinning out at their edges rather than ending in a hard dark mass; this is what tells the viewer at a glance that a battle happened here. Burn stays concentrated where the impacts are. EVERY BURNT MARK HAS A SOURCE YOU CAN SEE: burn only ever spreads outward from a crater or a ruined building and always touches it. NO BURNT STREAK, STRIPE OR PATCH STANDS ON ITS OWN in open ground or along a road with nothing at either end; ground with nothing to have burnt it stays clean. CRATERS ARE SHELL IMPACTS, NOT MOON CRATERS: never a scatter of matching round dimples, never a pockmarked lunar surface. Each is LARGE — wider than a whole carriageway — and irregular: a ragged asymmetric hole with a broken lip, a darker scorched pit inside and a fan of pale spoil thrown out to ONE side only. On a hard surface it punches through, radial cracks running out and pale gravel showing at the broken edge. They come in one or two tight groups, some cutting into each other.

MATERIALS READ APART AT A GLANCE: each has its own flat colour meeting its neighbour along a HARD CLEAN EDGE, never a blend. SAND — pale bleached ochre-grey, closer to bone than gold, never vivid yellow; BARE EARTH — duller grey-brown; CONCRETE — pale cool grey in large jointed panels; ASPHALT — medium neutral grey, darker than concrete, never near-black.

THERE ARE NO BUILDINGS AND NO ROADS IN THIS FIELD: no house, no apartment block, no office, no shop, no civilian structure of any kind, and no asphalt road, no kerb, no lane markings, no motorway and no street. Nothing here is a place people lived. Every built thing in the frame is a low military work — earth, sandbag, concrete or steel — laid out for use, not for living in.

MILITARY WORKS READ AS PLAN SHAPES, LOW AND WIDE, NEVER AS HOUSES: from straight above an earth revetment is a thick horseshoe or U of banked spoil with a flat floor inside it and a graded ramp at the open end; a bunker is a low slab of concrete mostly sunk into the ground, its roof a plain thick rectangle with a stepped entry trench cutting into one side and vent stacks on top; a sandbag position is a ring or short wall of small stacked blocks with a scalloped edge; a hardstand is a flat concrete apron of large slabs with straight joints and faded markings; a blast wall is a long straight low bank of banked earth or set concrete panels casting one narrow shadow. All of them are LOW: each throws a short shadow, none has storeys, none has windows, none has a pitched roof. They sit in working relationships — a revetment opens onto an apron, a trench runs into a bunker entry — never scattered decoratively and never in a tidy row. EVERY WORK IS BATTLE-DAMAGED, AND EACH DIFFERENTLY: a revetment breached with its bank spilled inward and its floor cratered; a bunker roof punched through into a ragged hole with the slab edges snapped and reinforcement bristling; a sandbag wall burst with its blocks spilled in a fan; an apron with slabs lifted, tilted and cracked open, pale gravel subgrade showing through the breaks; a blast wall knocked down over part of its length with the panels lying flat where they fell. Nothing is neatly sliced and nothing is left smooth — every break is ragged, bent and torn. No two works are damaged alike.

NO COMPLETE MACHINES ANYWHERE: this field contains no tank, no truck, no aircraft, no turret, no barrel and no vehicle of any kind, whole or wrecked — nothing that reads as a machine sitting on the ground. THE ONLY METAL IS BURIED SCRAP: ONE or TWO small torn fragments half sunk in the ground NEAR THE EDGE OF THE FRAME and never near the middle — a bent plate with ragged torn edges, or a buckled wheel rim on a snapped axle. Each is SMALL: shorter than a crater is wide and far smaller than the smallest building, burnt, deformed, mostly buried, and unreadable as part of any identifiable vehicle.

PLACEMENT: the few objects are spread thinly so no part of the frame is busier than another, with wide open ground between them — two things near each other, then a long gap, then one alone. Never a grid, rows, equal spacing, symmetry or one element per corner. The ground runs off all four edges; objects sit well in from the borders, drawn complete.
```
